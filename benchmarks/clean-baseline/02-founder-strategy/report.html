01

What the evidence says

The strongest near-term evidence is attached to the existing product and its implementation path. The new line has interest, but not yet commercial or delivery proof.

Current product: real usage, visible friction

  • 29 of 46 active workspaces used the March approvals workflow at least weekly last month.
  • 8 of 46 active workspaces have not completed onboarding.
  • Three accounts closed this quarter against an internal target of five. Two delayed decisions cited unclear implementation ownership; one cited price.
  • Gross revenue retention was 91% over the last twelve months; 74% of MRR comes from 18 accounts.

Supplier Analytics: a hypothesis, not a pipeline

  • Four interview requests indicate interest in a conversation.
  • There is no signed pilot, validated price, or delivery estimate in the supplied facts.
  • Support capacity is two engineers plus one implementation lead; next-quarter hiring is not approved.
  • A split would draw capacity away from a workflow already used weekly in 29 workspaces while implementation friction remains unresolved.
02

The two-quarter choice

Put the operating center of gravity on adoption.

Use the existing team to complete onboarding for the eight unfinished workspaces, clarify implementation ownership in the customer journey, and learn what separates weekly adopters from the rest. In parallel, allow only a small paid discovery test for Supplier Analytics. Do not commit a build team until a customer pays and scope, price, and delivery effort become testable.

Do not treat interviews as demand validation or assume that adoption work will improve retention or sales until measured.
03

Sequence the work

Quarter 1 · Instrument and unblock

Make onboarding ownership explicit

  • Assign a named owner and clear handoff for each onboarding step using current capacity.
  • Work through the 8 unfinished workspaces; record completion and blockers.
  • Track weekly approvals use across the 46 active workspaces with a consistent definition.
  • Run a paid discovery offer for Supplier Analytics only if a buyer accepts a defined, bounded scope.

Quarter 2 · Review evidence and choose

Deepen what is working; gate expansion

  • Compare onboarding completion and weekly use with the starting cohort; document the method and counts.
  • Review implementation-related sales delays and whether ownership changes addressed them.
  • For the new line, require paid commitment plus validated scope, price, and delivery estimate before proposing build capacity.
  • Revisit the allocation and runway implications with observed results; do not presume hiring approval.
04

Make the opportunity cost explicit

Adoption focusUses scarce engineering and implementation capacity on the shipped workflow, the 8 incomplete onboardings, and implementation ownership friction associated with two delayed sales decisions.
What gets deferredA broader Supplier Analytics build and any assumption of near-term revenue from that line. Four interview requests do not establish willingness to pay or delivery feasibility.
Cost of a splitLess concentrated attention on both onboarding and product discovery, with the existing team already limited to two engineers and one implementation lead and no approved next-quarter hiring budget.
Cost of adoption-onlySlower learning about Supplier Analytics. A small paid test preserves some learning while limiting the risk of assigning a team to an unvalidated offer.
05

Signals and assumptions stay separate

Measured in the source Evidence

Runway, MRR, account concentration, trailing twelve-month gross revenue retention, weekly approvals usage, incomplete onboarding count, quarterly closes versus internal target, stated reasons for two delays and one price objection, interview requests, available support capacity, and hiring-budget status.

To test, not assume Unknown

Whether completing onboarding increases weekly usage; whether deeper usage changes retention or expansion; whether clearer implementation ownership shortens sales decisions; whether any Supplier Analytics interview request converts to a paid pilot; and what the new line would cost to deliver.

06

Decision rule

Continue the adoption emphasis while onboarding work and usage are being measured. Consider allocating build capacity to Supplier Analytics only after a paid commitment establishes buyer willingness, and after the scope, price, and delivery estimate are documented. If these conditions do not arrive, keep the line at discovery level and preserve the team's focus.
